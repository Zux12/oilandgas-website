What Operators Check Before Taking a Plant Back from a Project Team

Oil & Gas Editorial

Published on Oil & Gas: 2026-11-27

Summary

October 2026 edition · Mechanical completion records what has been built. Handover also has to show that the people accepting the plant understand its operating limits, outstanding work and new responsibilities.

Full article

A contractor has completed a modification and cleared the work area. Most items on the punch list are closed. The project team is ready to declare completion, and operations is about to take custody of the equipment. Both statements can be accurate while leaving one important question open. What evidence supports the next authorised operating state?

FOLLOW ONE CHANGE THROUGH THE RECORDS

Consider a replacement control valve whose failure position changed during detailed design. The revised drawing is one piece of the record. Operations also needs the approved design basis, the relevant test results and an explanation of what the change means for operating and emergency procedures. A test marked “pass” is only meaningful against the requirement it was expected to demonstrate.

For processes covered by the US OSHA process safety management standard, the pre-startup review includes specified checks on equipment, procedures, hazard review and training. Other jurisdictions and facilities have their own requirements. The practical question for a handover meeting is whether the evidence required for this installation has reached the people authorised to accept it.

OPEN ITEMS HAVE DIFFERENT CONSEQUENCES

A missing label on a spare cabinet and an unresolved protection-function test may both be recorded as one open punch item. Counting them gives little guidance about readiness. The team must understand each item’s effect and who has authority to decide whether it prevents the next step. Where a limitation is accepted, it needs an owner and a clear boundary.

Try asking the incoming shift to explain the change using the documents available at the control room. If its operators still depend on a project engineer’s personal notebook to identify a temporary condition, the handover package is incomplete in a practical sense. Closing that information gap while the project team is present is easier than reconstructing the decision later.

HAND OVER THE EXCEPTIONS

A project may have completed its design changes while a temporary instruction still governs part of the commissioning work. The receiving supervisor needs to know which instruction applies at handover, when it expires and who is authorised to withdraw it. An unsigned copy left in a folder can create doubt precisely when the team needs a clear answer. Walking through one such exception together is a practical way to test whether the transfer of responsibility is understood on both sides.

TAKEAWAY

A useful handover makes three things clear to the receiving team. What changed, what proves it performs as intended, and what responsibility is being transferred now? The signature then records a decision that the next shift can understand.
