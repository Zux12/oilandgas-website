What the Outgoing Shift Knows That the Logbook Can Miss

Oil & Gas Editorial

Published on Oil & Gas: 2026-11-27

Summary

November 2026 edition · An entry can be factually correct and still leave the next shift with the wrong expectation. A useful handover makes unfinished work, uncertainty and the next decision explicit.

Full article

“Standby pump available” is a short, reassuring log entry. Imagine that the machine has returned from maintenance and its electrical checks are complete, but the site’s required run test remains outstanding. The outgoing operator understands exactly what the entry meant. The incoming operator could reasonably take a different meaning from the same three words.

THE GAP BETWEEN STATE AND EXPECTATION

A shift log records events. The team going off duty also carries a picture of what it expected to happen next. That picture can include a laboratory result due after midnight, a temporary operating restriction or an instrument indication that has yet to be reconciled with field observations. If the next shift receives only the completed actions, it can lose the context that made them intelligible.

The UK Health and Safety Executive describes effective handover as an exchange that relies on preparation, communication and cross-checking. Written information preserves continuity. A two-way conversation gives the receiving team an opportunity to question an ambiguous statement while the people who wrote it are still available.

GIVE THE NEXT SHIFT A DECISION IT CAN RECOGNISE

Consider a tank transfer awaiting a quality result. A useful entry identifies the tank and batch, the outstanding result, who is expected to communicate it and what authorisation will be required before the next movement. It also says where the controlling record can be found. This level of specificity is especially important when several tanks have similar names or when shifts change during simultaneous work.

One useful cross-check is to ask the incoming person to describe the next significant decision in their own words. A different answer can expose an ambiguity without anyone having to wait for a mistake. It also helps distinguish a routine item that can be read later from an unresolved condition that deserves discussion now.

WHAT BELONGS AT THE TOP OF THE PAGE

A long list of completed jobs can bury the one item that still changes how the plant may be operated. Give that item a visible place in the handover and connect it to the relevant permit, isolation or operating instruction. At the following shift change, review whether it still applies before copying it forward. Otherwise a stale warning can acquire the appearance of a permanent condition, while a genuinely new restriction receives the same familiar wording.

TAKEAWAY

Write the fact, the remaining uncertainty and the next owner. The best handover leaves the incoming shift able to explain both what the plant is doing and which assumption it must check before taking the next step.
